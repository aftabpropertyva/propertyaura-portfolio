# PropertyAura Competitor Audit and First-Page Strategy

**Prepared:** 3 November 2026  
**Target queries:** `UK land value calculator`, `residual land value calculator`, `buy to let business plan UK`, `buy to let cash flow calculator`

## Main conclusion

PropertyAura is competing in two different search markets. The land-value market is led by pages that provide a specialised calculator, scenario testing, worked examples and access to planning or comparable-sales data. The buy-to-let market is led by pages that combine a practical business-plan framework with detailed financial examples, tax context, calculators, trust signals and commercial next steps.

PropertyAura already has a good technical foundation and a working calculator. The biggest gap is not another meta tag. It is stronger evidence, more scenario depth, clearer topical coverage and more authority links from relevant property sources.

## Competitor 1: Value My Land

[Value My Land](https://valuemyland.uk/) uses a highly focused landing page. It asks for the land postcode, optionally improves the result with a mapped area, captures acreage and asks for an email address before delivering an immediate estimate. It reinforces conversion with short trust statements such as free of charge, instant quote, daily-updated pricing and experienced consultants.

### What to learn

The page has one clear job: get a valuation lead. The form is specific to land, and the promised output is concrete. The trust statements reduce uncertainty about why a visitor should submit details.

### What PropertyAura should do

Create a clearly labelled “Get an expert review” path beside the free calculator. Ask only for useful information such as postcode, site area and email, explain exactly what the user will receive, and include a consent statement. Do not promise a professional valuation unless a qualified valuer is actually providing one. PropertyAura can differentiate by providing the calculation instantly and offering an optional review request separately.

## Competitor 2: Atlasly

[Atlasly’s residual land value calculator](https://atlasly.app/tools/residual-land-value-calculator) exposes the assumptions that matter: GDV, construction cost per square metre, gross floor area, site area, professional fees, contingency, finance and developer profit. It also explains what its simplified model excludes, including land purchase costs, tax, sales costs, planning contributions, infrastructure and abnormal costs.

### What to learn

The calculator is compact but professionally framed. It avoids pretending to be a valuation and clearly states how percentages are applied. Its next-step call to action moves users from a free tool into a fuller viability-appraisal product.

### What PropertyAura should do

Add floor area, site area and a sensitivity table to the PropertyAura tool. Show base, downside and upside residual values. Add a clear “what this excludes” section. Link the tool to an optional feasibility-review enquiry rather than using an unsupported valuation claim.

## Competitor 3: Searchland

[Searchland’s development-land guide](https://searchland.co.uk/blog/how-to-value-development-land) is the strongest content competitor for the land topic. It covers three methods: residual land value, comparable evidence and benchmark land value. It includes a worked example with GDV, construction, fees, finance, sales costs, planning contributions, contingency, developer profit and land value per acre. It also explains common pitfalls such as optimism bias, site constraints, planning risk, market timing and weak sensitivity analysis.

The page then connects the educational content to proprietary data features covering sales comparables, planning context, market analysis and cost benchmarking.

### What to learn

This is a complete topic cluster, not just a calculator page. It answers the definition, method, example, risks, data requirements and next action. The article also benefits from a named author, publication date and a product ecosystem.

### What PropertyAura should do

Expand the land page with three valuation methods, a worked example, sensitivity analysis and a due-diligence checklist. Add official and professional sources where appropriate. Build related pages for residual land value, development appraisal, planning risk and comparable evidence, then link them together.

## Competitor 4: EVO Property Management

[EVO’s buy-to-let business-plan guide](https://evo-pm.com/insights/the-ultimate-buy-to-let-strategy-and-business-plan/) directly targets the business-plan intent. It uses a contents list and a four-step structure covering finances, market research, finding tenants and property management. It includes practical cost categories, mortgage comparisons, rental-yield maths and decisions about management and ownership.

### What to learn

The guide matches the query language closely. It does not only discuss whether buy-to-let is profitable; it explains how to build a plan and what sections the plan should contain.

### What PropertyAura should do

Keep the new business-plan section in the BTL guide, but add a downloadable or printable business-plan template, a worked monthly cashflow example and a simple contents list near the top. Connect every section to one calculator or checklist.

## Competitor 5: UK Landlord Tax

[UK Landlord Tax’s buy-to-let mortgage calculator](https://uklandlordtax.co.uk/calculators/buy-to-let-mortgage-calculator/) combines an interest-only and repayment comparison with monthly payment outputs, total paid over the term, tax explanations, FAQs, related guides and a prominent expert-contact path.

### What to learn

The page answers the immediate calculator question and the next questions a serious visitor has: tax, allowable costs, company ownership, mortgage structure and professional advice. It also contains strong commercial trust signals and clear disclaimers.

### What PropertyAura should do

Add a visible “next step” after calculator results: save assumptions, read the relevant guide, or request a review. Add FAQs below important tools and show both the number and its interpretation. Keep the educational disclaimer prominent.

## Competitor 6: Total Landlord Insurance

[Total Landlord Insurance’s investment guide](https://www.totallandlordinsurance.co.uk/knowledge-centre/the-ultimate-guide-to-buy-to-let-property-investment) uses an established brand, broad coverage, risk discussion and links into related landlord topics. Its content covers investment objectives, time horizon, risk tolerance, landlord involvement, reserves, tenant demand and compliance.

### What to learn

Authority comes from the surrounding site, the regulated business identity, related resources and practical risk discussion. The page is not dependent on one keyword or one calculator.

### What PropertyAura should do

Build a connected landlord knowledge centre. Add an author profile, “last reviewed” dates, source links, a clear editorial disclaimer and links to official guidance. Publish supporting articles around MTD, HMO licensing, SDLT, Section 24 and landlord compliance.

## Competitive strategy for PropertyAura

### Phase 1: Strengthen the pages already targeting impressions

1. Keep the new [Land Value Calculator](https://aftabpropertyva.github.io/propertyaura-portfolio/land-value-calculator.html) as the primary target for `UK land value calculator`.
2. Add a worked example using clearly labelled illustrative assumptions.
3. Add base, downside and upside sensitivity results.
4. Add the three valuation methods: residual, comparable evidence and benchmark.
5. Add a “what this calculator excludes” box.
6. Keep the BTL guide’s new business-plan section and add a contents list near the top.
7. Add a downloadable business-plan worksheet or printable HTML template.

### Phase 2: Build a topic cluster

Create and internally link these pages:

- Residual land value calculator
- How to value development land
- UK buy-to-let business-plan template
- Buy-to-let cashflow calculator
- Rental yield and ICR explained
- Section 24 tax and cashflow guide
- HMO investment appraisal
- SDLT for additional properties

Every page should link to the calculator, the guide hub and at least two related pages. This gives Google a clear subject relationship rather than a collection of isolated pages.

### Phase 3: Add trust and conversion signals

Add an author block to substantial guides, state when each article was last reviewed, link tax and compliance claims to official sources, and make the disclaimer clear. Add a single relevant call to action after each tool: download the worksheet, request an educational review or read the next guide.

### Phase 4: Earn authority links

The competitors with the strongest visibility have either established brands, proprietary data or commercial ecosystems. PropertyAura needs relevant mentions. Promote the free calculator through UK landlord communities, property-investor LinkedIn posts, local landlord associations, broker contacts and guest articles. Do not buy bulk links or copy competitor content.

## What not to copy

Do not copy competitor wording, examples, layouts, images or branding. Do not make unsupported claims about instant valuations, tax savings or mortgage approval. Use the competitors’ structure as a benchmark, then publish original calculations, original examples and transparent assumptions.

## Priority order

1. Add sensitivity testing and a worked example to the land calculator.
2. Add a printable buy-to-let business-plan worksheet.
3. Add author, review date and official-source links to major guides.
4. Publish two supporting pages each month.
5. Earn relevant backlinks and monitor Search Console queries weekly.
