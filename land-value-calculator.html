How residual land value works

The residual method starts with the expected gross development value (GDV) and deducts the costs required to deliver and sell the project. The amount left is an indication of the maximum land value before negotiation, risk adjustments and any acquisition-specific costs.

The core formula is:

GDV − build costs − fees − contingency − finance − sales costs − target profit = indicative residual land value

What should be included?

Build costs should reflect the actual specification and current contractor evidence. Professional fees can include design, planning, surveying, project management and other consultants. Contingency is a risk allowance, not free profit. Finance should cover interest and relevant lender fees for the expected programme. Sales costs may include agents, marketing and legal work.

Use several scenarios rather than relying on one number. Test a lower GDV, higher build cost, longer programme and higher interest cost. A site that only works under optimistic assumptions may not justify the same land price as a resilient scheme.

Land development due diligence

  • Confirm planning status, constraints and access.
  • Check utilities, ground conditions and abnormal works.
  • Obtain comparable evidence for the completed value.
  • Ask a quantity surveyor to test the build budget.
  • Allow for programme delays and finance sensitivity.
  • Review tax, acquisition costs and ownership structure separately.

For rental projects, continue with the UK property calculators and read the buy-to-let investment guide before committing capital.

Is this a land valuation?

No. The calculator is a quick feasibility tool for comparing assumptions. A land value depends on planning risk, the site’s location, legal title, access, abnormal ground conditions, market demand, the proposed scheme and the price a specific buyer is prepared to pay. A professional valuation and planning-led appraisal are still required.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GDV and land value?

GDV is the expected value of the completed development. Land value is only the residual amount left after the development costs and target return are deducted.

Can I use this for a buy-to-let scheme?

Yes, as an initial estimate, but a rental scheme needs a different appraisal approach. Include the completed property value or refinance assumptions, rental cashflow, finance structure and exit strategy rather than relying only on a sales GDV.
